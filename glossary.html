Jargon decoder

Every acronym you are likely to meet, with the expansion and one plain sentence explaining what it is for. Type anything — an acronym, a word from a definition, or a concept — to filter the list.

 

  • A/Daccumulation and distributionThe building of a position quietly, and the unloading of it into demand later. The bookends of every move in this framework.
  • ADRaverage daily rangeHow far this market typically travels in a day. Used to size stops and to judge whether a day has already done its work.
  • AKZAsian killzone20:00–00:00 New York time. Usually consolidation; its high and low become the first targets for London.
  • AMDaccumulation, manipulation, distributionThe three phases of a move: build quietly, fake the other way, then deliver. Same thing as power of three.
  • AM session09:30–12:00The New York morning range. Referenced in the afternoon when looking for a reversal profile.
  • ATMthe ATM methodA standalone 1-hour pattern: key extreme, prior swing broken, retrace into the breaker, enter there.
  • BBbreaker blockAn order block that failed and flipped sides after a stop run. Old resistance becomes support, or vice versa.
  • BEbreak-evenMoving the stop to your entry price. Useful, but moving it too early is a documented way to turn winners into scratches.
  • BGbreakaway gapA gap left at the start of a strong move that is deliberately not filled. Read as confirmation the move is real.
  • BISIbuyside imbalance, sellside inefficiencyA fair value gap created by a fast move up. Sits below price and is a bullish, discount-side level.
  • BMS / BOSbreak of market structure / break of structurePrice makes a new high in an uptrend or a new low in a downtrend — continuation, not reversal.
  • BPRbalanced price rangeA stretch traded through in both directions, so both buying and selling have been offered there. Considered finished business.
  • BSLbuyside liquidityBuy orders resting above highs — mostly the stops of short positions. A target for anyone needing to sell size.
  • CBDRcentral bank dealers rangeThe 14:00–20:00 range. Its height is projected as equal steps above and below to estimate the next day's high and low.
  • CEconsequent encroachmentThe midpoint of any gap or void. Watched as closely as the gap's edges.
  • CPEclose proximity entriesEntering near the session's opening price rather than at the extreme, used when you missed the first entry or want to add.
  • DDdrawdownHow far your account, or an open position, has fallen from its peak.
  • DH / DLdaily high / daily lowThe extremes of the current day.
  • Displacement—A fast, large, decisive move that closes beyond a level and leaves a gap. The quality check on every structural signal.
  • DOLdraw on liquidityThe level you believe price is currently travelling toward. The destination, not the entry.
  • DXYUS dollar indexThe dollar measured against a basket of currencies. Usually moves inversely to equity index futures; used as a cross-check.
  • EQequilibriumThe 50% level of a range. Fair value. Above it is premium, below it is discount.
  • EQH / EQLequal highs / equal lowsTwo or more touches at nearly the same price. The flattest levels attract the most stops and make the biggest pools.
  • ERLexternal range liquidityThe old high and old low that define a range. Stops sit outside them.
  • ESE-mini S&P 500 futuresThe index futures contract most of this material's examples are drawn from. MES is the micro version.
  • FOMCFederal Open Market CommitteeThe US rate-setting meetings. The 14:00 release and the press conference after it routinely move opposite ways; widely treated as a day to sit out.
  • FVGfair value gapA three-candle formation where the outer wicks do not overlap. The untouched space is expected to be revisited.
  • HH / HLhigher high / higher lowThe building blocks of an uptrend in ordinary technical language.
  • HOD / LODhigh of day / low of dayToday's extremes. Which session forms them is a large part of the daily analysis.
  • HOW / LOWhigh of week / low of weekThe week's extremes. Usually set Tuesday or Wednesday.
  • HRLRhigh resistance liquidity runLiquidity that price has to fight through structure to reach. Slow, choppy, and to be avoided as a target.
  • HTF / LTFhigher timeframe / lower timeframeHigher sets direction and destination; lower times the entry. When they disagree, higher wins.
  • IDRinterbank dealing rangeA range with both sides already taken, used as the container for measuring premium and discount.
  • IFVGinversion fair value gapA gap price traded straight through instead of respecting. It flips polarity: a failed bullish gap becomes resistance.
  • Inside bar—A candle contained entirely within the previous one. A pause. On index futures, consecutive inside days make a raid of the prior day's extreme more likely.
  • IOFinstitutional order flowReading whether the market is in a buying or selling program, chiefly by watching whether up-close or down-close candles are being respected.
  • IOFEDinstitutional order flow entry drillAn entry routine that uses a gap left inside the leg that broke structure, rather than waiting for a deeper retracement.
  • IPDAinterbank price delivery algorithmThe framework's name for whatever is assumed to be delivering price. In practice it means the 20/40/60-day look-back exercise.
  • IRLinternal range liquidityThe unfilled gaps and blocks inside a range. Price alternates between these and the range's outer edges.
  • ITH / ITLintermediate-term high / lowA swing point with lower highs (or higher lows) on both sides of it. Breaking one is a genuine structural event.
  • Judas swing—The first move of a session, deliberately in the wrong direction, that sets the day's extreme before the real move.
  • KZkillzoneA window when a session opens or two overlap, and therefore when volume and setups arrive.
  • LC / LCKZLondon close / London close killzone10:00–12:00. Profit-taking as London squares up. Often a retracement, sometimes a full reversal of the day.
  • LH / LLlower high / lower lowThe building blocks of a downtrend.
  • LO / LOKZLondon open / London open killzone02:00–05:00. The highest-energy window and the most likely place for the day's high or low.
  • LRLRlow resistance liquidity runUntouched orders with a clear path to them. Fast and clean — the conditions you actually want to target.
  • LTH / LTLlong-term high / lowAn intermediate-term swing point with lower intermediate-term highs (or higher lows) on both sides. Usually a daily-chart level.
  • LVliquidity voidA stretch of one-sided delivery — long bodies, tiny wicks — where almost no two-way trading happened. Expected to be revisited.
  • MBmitigation blockLike a breaker, but the swing failed to reach the previous extreme, so no stops were run. Same trade, weaker premise.
  • MGmeasuring gapAn unfilled gap near the midpoint of a move, used to project where the move ends.
  • MMBM / MMSMmarket maker buy model / sell modelThe full round-trip curve: a program in one direction to engineer liquidity, a reversal, then the return trip to collect it.
  • MMXMmarket maker buy/sell modelUmbrella label for the two curves above.
  • MNO / NMONew York midnight opening priceThe 00:00 price. Kept on the chart all day as the reference that separates premium from discount for the session.
  • MS / MSSmarket structure / market structure shiftA shift is a break in the opposite direction to the prevailing pattern, with displacement, shortly after a liquidity raid. The reversal signal.
  • MTmean thresholdThe 50% level of an order block's body. A close beyond it is usually treated as invalidation.
  • NDOGnew day opening gapThe space between the 17:00 close and the 18:00 open. A genuine void, since no trading happened between the two prices.
  • NFPnon-farm payrollsThe monthly US jobs report, released the first Friday of the month. The Thursday and Friday of that week are widely avoided.
  • NQE-mini Nasdaq 100 futuresThe other main index contract used in examples. MNQ is the micro version.
  • NWOGnew week opening gapThe space between Friday's close and Sunday's open. Kept on the chart for weeks; price returns to these long after they form.
  • NYO / NYOKZNew York open / New York open killzone07:00–10:00. Either a continuation of London or a clean reversal, depending on where London finished.
  • OBorder blockThe last opposite-colour candle before a decisive move. Measured body-only, and watched on the retest.
  • OForder flowWhich side is being respected right now. Bullish means down-close candles hold; bearish means up-close candles cap price.
  • Opening range09:30–10:00The first half hour after the equity open. Defines the initial support and resistance for the session.
  • OTEoptimal trade entryA preferred slice of a retracement, roughly the 62%–79% pullback, for entering in the direction of the larger move.
  • PAprice actionReading the chart itself — highs, lows, opens, closes — rather than derived indicators.
  • PD arraypremium/discount arrayCatalogue term for any specific level where a reaction is expected: gaps, blocks, breakers, voids, opening gaps.
  • PDH / PDLprevious day's high / lowThe most-watched intraday levels. The market reaches for one of them most days.
  • PM session13:30–16:00The afternoon range. Yesterday's PM high and low are prime targets for this morning.
  • PO3power of threeAccumulation, manipulation, distribution applied to a single candle at any timeframe. The wick is the manipulation.
  • POIpoint of interestGeneric term for a level you are watching. Usually a PD array.
  • Propulsion block—An order block formed inside an older order block. Sensitive: it should hold above its own midpoint.
  • PWH / PWLprevious week's high / lowThe weekly equivalent of PDH and PDL, and a common draw for a multi-day move.
  • RBrejection blockBuilt from wicks rather than bodies: the space between the highest body and the wick tops at a spiky swing extreme.
  • RDRBredelivered rebalanced PD arrayA range delivered up, down and up again (or the reverse). Both sides offered, so it is treated as complete.
  • RHOreturn to originPrice coming back to the block or level a move started from.
  • RR / R:Rrisk-to-reward ratioHow much you stand to make against how much you are risking, measured before entry, not after.
  • RTH / ETHregular trading hours / electronic trading hoursWhich futures session your chart displays. It matters: opening gaps are only visible on the regular-hours view.
  • S&Dseek and destroyA day profile that sweeps both sides of the range and trends nowhere. Common around news and on Thursdays of payrolls week.
  • SBsilver bulletA time-boxed setup: within a fixed one-hour window, usually 10:00–11:00, take the first clean gap after a liquidity raid.
  • SDR / BDRsellside / buyside delivery rangeWithin a market maker model, the leg from the point of origin to the target, graded into quarters.
  • SIBIsellside imbalance, buyside inefficiencyA fair value gap created by a fast move down. Sits above price and is a bearish, premium-side level.
  • SL / TPstop loss / take profitThe exits. In this framework both are placed at structural levels, not at round numbers or fixed distances.
  • SMRsmart money reversalThe turn at the top or bottom of a market maker curve, where a buy program becomes a sell program or vice versa.
  • SMTsmart money technique / SMT divergenceTwo correlated markets disagreeing at a level — one makes a new extreme, the other does not. A confirmation, never a trigger on its own.
  • SSLsellside liquiditySell orders resting below lows — mostly the stops of long positions. A target for anyone needing to buy size.
  • STH / STLshort-term high / lowA basic three-candle swing point. Noise on its own; used for entry timing.
  • Stop hunt—Price moving to a level specifically to trigger the orders resting there, then leaving.
  • Swing high / low—A candle whose high is higher (or low is lower) than the candles immediately either side of it. Colours do not matter.
  • TSturtle soupThe trade against a failed breakout: price pokes beyond an old extreme, fails, closes back inside, and you enter on the failure.
  • Terminus—The end point of a market maker curve — the liquidity the whole round trip was built to reach.
  • VIvolume imbalanceTwo candle bodies that do not touch although their wicks overlap. A weaker relative of the fair value gap.
  • Vacuum block—A literal price gap left by a news release or a session open. Often filled the same session, but not always.
  • YME-mini Dow futuresThe third index contract commonly used alongside ES and NQ for correlation checks.

Terms that mean the same thing

Several pairs are used interchangeably, which makes the vocabulary look larger than it is:

  • Smart Money Concepts, SMC and smart money — the same material.
  • Imbalance, inefficiency and fair value gap — usually the same object.
  • Stop hunt, liquidity raid, liquidity sweep and purge — the same event.
  • Rebalance and fill the gap — the same action.
  • Break of market structure, BMS and BOS — the same label, used inconsistently for both continuation and reversal breaks. Check which one a given writer means.

Prop accounts

A funded account is not judged on whether it made money. It is judged against two numbers somebody else set — a target that passes it and a drawdown that ends it. Your journal already knows where every account sits between them; it just needs telling what the two numbers are.

Read this before trusting the drawdown figure

It depends which drawdown you bought, and the two are not close. An intraday trailing drawdown follows your equity while a position is open, unrealised profit included — so a trade that ran $800 in your favour and closed at $200 moved the threshold by $800, and the journal only ever saw the $200. An end-of-day drawdown does not move until the day closes, and a spike that gives itself back never touches it.

Say which one each account is, on the card. Everything below is computed from closed trades, and what that is worth depends entirely on the answer:

  • Intraday — the drawdown shown is a floor. The real one is at least this bad and can be considerably worse, because the peak here is the lowest the real peak can be.
  • End of day — the closing balances this page holds are the numbers the firm used, so the figure is exact, provided every trade is imported and nothing was carried overnight.

An account nobody has classified is treated as intraday, which is the pessimistic reading rather than the accurate one.

On an intraday account, use it to see a trend and never to decide whether the account is still alive — the firm's own dashboard is the only thing that can answer that.

“Room left” leans the other way, and on an intraday account that is the dangerous one. It is the same arithmetic read forwards — the drawdown less the fall from the high-water mark, which your firm calls distance to drawdown. Because an intraday peak here is the lowest the real peak can be, the room shown is the most you could possibly have: a higher real peak means a higher floor and less room than this says. It can tell you that you are in trouble; it can never tell you that you are safe. On an end-of-day account it can do both.

Adding an account

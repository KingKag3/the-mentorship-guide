Prop accounts

A funded account is not judged on whether it made money. It is judged against two numbers somebody else set — a target that passes it and a drawdown that ends it. Your journal already knows where every account sits between them; it just needs telling what the two numbers are.

Read this before trusting the drawdown figure

Your journal holds closed trades. A trailing drawdown does not. Apex and most firms trail against your account's high-water mark including open profit, intraday — so a trade that ran $800 in your favour and closed at $200 moved the threshold by $800, and the journal only ever saw the $200.

Everything below computes from closed results, which makes the drawdown shown a floor: the real one is at least this bad and can be considerably worse. Use it to see a trend, never to decide whether an account is still alive. The firm's own dashboard is the only thing that can answer that.

“Room left” leans the other way, and that is the dangerous one. It is the same arithmetic read forwards — the drawdown less the fall from the high-water mark, which your firm calls distance to drawdown. Because this peak is the lowest the real peak can be, the room shown is the most you could possibly have. A higher real peak means a higher floor and less room than this says. It can tell you that you are in trouble; it can never tell you that you are safe.

Adding an account

Any account name that appears in your journal shows up below automatically. Fill in what the firm set and it starts tracking. Nothing here is filled in for you except the Apex profit target, which is offered when you pick a size and can be overwritten.
